2025/26 Season Review

Performance, Development & Competitive Context

The 2025/26 season marked my first appointment as Head Coach in the easyCredit BBL, leading Basketball Löwen Braunschweig across two competitions: the German BBL and the FIBA Europe Cup.

The campaign included a 7–7 combined record during the initial competitive phase, a 4–2 record in the FIBA Europe Cup first round and six consecutive home victories across competitions. The later BBL stretch was 1–9. These are distinct phases of the season and are presented separately rather than combined into an overall record.

The team had one of the youngest competitive profiles in the two competitions: the second-youngest roster in the BBL and the youngest roster in the FIBA Europe Cup. Five young players were integrated into the competitive setup: one U22 player, three U20 players and one U17 player.

01 · Phase One

Pre-FIBA Break: Establishing Competitiveness

The opening phase established a competitive foundation across two competitions. The team recorded a combined 7–7 record, including a 4–2 record in the FIBA Europe Cup first round, while building a six-game home winning streak across competitions.

This was achieved with one of the youngest competitive structures in the two competitions, while the team was already managing significant frontcourt absences.

The team was already dealing with significant frontcourt absences. As established players became unavailable, Romario Holloway and Eliah Braun stepped into increased roles, providing important internal alternatives and helping the team maintain competitive continuity.

The six-game home winning sequence included important results across both competitions. The team defeated Rostock 92–71 in the BBL and Bonn 89–80 in the BBL, while also recording FIBA Europe Cup wins over PAOK and Anwil during the wider opening phase.

Turning Point · November FIBA Break

Losing the Solutions to the Original Problems

The FIBA break marked a significant turning point in the season. The team was progressively losing not only established rotation players, but also the younger internal alternatives who had stepped forward to compensate for the original absences.

Romario Holloway and Eliah Braun, who had taken on increased responsibilities during the initial period of frontcourt shortages, also became unavailable through injury. The team was therefore losing both original rotation options and some of the solutions developed to cover their absence.

02 · Phase Two

Post-FIBA Break: Loss of Competitive Continuity

Following the break, further absences progressively reduced the available rotation. The impact extended beyond the number of players available on game day: it also affected the team's ability to prepare under representative BBL-level practice conditions.

With limited frontcourt availability, it became increasingly difficult to reproduce the size, physicality and positional matchups normally encountered in BBL competition during daily practice. This placed additional demands on game preparation, tactical adjustments and the workload carried by the remaining players.

The availability figures reflect this progression. Overall roster availability declined from 79% in the opening competitive phase to 71% in the later period. Frontcourt availability fell from 64% to 54%.

The later BBL stretch finished with a 1–9 record. During this period, roster availability, frontcourt balance and the conditions for representative preparation had changed substantially.

03 · Roster Profile

Player Development Within Competition

The team operated with the second-youngest roster in the BBL and the youngest roster in the FIBA Europe Cup. Five young players were integrated into the competitive setup: one U22 player, three U20 players and one U17 player.

As established players became unavailable at different stages, several of these younger players assumed greater responsibilities within the rotation. Their involvement formed part of the team's competitive structure across the two competitions.

The age profile and player integration are included here as elements of the season's roster and development picture, alongside the results and availability data.

04 · Coaching Work

Adaptation & Decision-Making

Across the campaign, coaching work included adjustments to rotations, tactical roles, game plans and practice workloads as the personnel available changed. The focus was on maintaining clear responsibilities, defensive activity and competitive standards within the rotation available for each game.

These adjustments were part of the day-to-day coaching process across a two-competition schedule. They are presented as the practical coaching response to the evolving team and roster situation, rather than as an explanation for any single result.
